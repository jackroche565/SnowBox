# Snowbox

Snowfall and forecasts for ski areas across the US and Canada, in regions (Northeast first). (First called Snowline; the repo is now `SnowBox`.) Next.js (App Router, TypeScript, Tailwind v4), deployed on Vercel. The owner is new to coding: explain things plainly and keep changes focused.

## Deploying

Vercel auto-deploys the branch `claude/web-app-github-vercel-p9ocy4` as production. Pushing to it updates the live site (www.snowbox.app; Vercel project "snowbox" in the jack-r-skis team, also at snowbox-jack-r-skis.vercel.app). Other branches get Vercel preview URLs. Run `git pull` before starting work, since cloud and local sessions both push here.

## Before pushing

Run `npm run lint`, `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npm run build`. All must pass.

## Structure

- Each tab has one job. `/` Home: on a first visit, "Which mountains do you ski?" with a region dropdown and that region's states, which expand to starred rows (search covers every region); after that, one headline number (most snow in the next 7 days, or the next snow) and the "Your week" grid: your mountains as rows, the next 7 days as columns, a bar per day sized by its snow, plus a total. Edit opens the picker. `/explore`: a region dropdown and pass dropdown, then map-first on phones (Map/List text tabs in the header), list beside the map on desktop; the map frames the chosen region; tapping a dot opens a bottom panel with View mountain. Leads to `/resorts/[id]` (one page, no tabs, statically generated): spec strip, Snow (last 48 hrs, next 3 days, modeled base), Next 7 days as a bar chart with the days 8–16 total and plain sentences for the storm, refreeze, wind holds and opening date, hour by hour folded away, then links (official lifts & trails report first). `/decide`: where to ski on a day, two simple ways, within the chosen region. A region dropdown, a day strip, then text toggles at the top: Most snow | Closest (never blended; Closest needs a starting point), the pass dropdown, and "From Boston · Change". Most snow ranks by that day's new snow only (ties, including no snow at all, go closest first); drive time, passes, rain and wind holds sit beside each mountain. Mountains that aren't open yet stay in the list, greyed out with their opening date, so Decide works out of season. No blended score, drive limits, compare or demo data: keep it that simple. `/compare` redirects to `/decide` (in `next.config.ts`).
- Nothing unnecessary: people have limited time and attention. Before adding anything, ask whether it repeats something already on screen or makes someone stop and decode it; if so, leave it out. One entry point per action.
- Copy rules: the only title is "Snowbox" in the bar. No generated or changing headlines ("Storm Watch" and the like), no marketing sentences. Section labels are small uppercase labels, not big headings. State numbers plainly.
- Navigation: `src/components/Nav.tsx` holds the tabs: top tabs in the header from tablet width up, and a bottom tab bar on phones (the root layout pads the page for it).
- `src/data/resorts.json` holds 301 downhill areas in nine regions (`src/lib/regions.ts`: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Rockies, Sierra & California, Pacific Northwest, Alaska, Western Canada, Eastern Canada; each mountain's `region` is set by its state or province). The rule for what's listed (Oct 2026): real, public, outdoor, lift-served mountains people actually go to. Every mountain on Epic, Ikon (including its bonus mountains) or Mountain Collective; any other area (Indy, Snow Pass or independent) with at least 500 ft of vertical; and below 500 ft only busy, well-known regional areas (Nashoba Valley, Blue Hills, Mount Southington, Powder Ridge CT, Peek'n Peak, Bear Creek, Snow Ridge, Crystal Mountain MI, Nub's Nob, Big Powderhorn, Chestnut Mountain, Cascade Mountain, Trollhaugen, Hyland Hills, Howelson Hill, Yawgoo Valley) plus Moose Mountain and Skeetawk (real Alaska mountains whose vertical is missing). No indoor slopes (Big SNOW was removed), kids' lesson programs (National Winter Activity Center) or small community tows; 75 such hills were removed in Oct 2026. Vertical for most non-New-England areas is an OpenSkiMap estimate, so check a borderline one before adding or dropping it. The original 19 New England areas were hand-checked; the rest came from OpenSkiMap (location, lift-served top and bottom elevation flagged as estimates, homepage) and were matched to the passes' own 2026-27 lists in Oct 2026 (Indy by its site's coordinates). OpenSkiMap's run counts are trail segments, not trails, so trail counts come only from the resorts themselves. Members-only clubs are left out. Each mountain also has `country` (US/CA) and `timezone`. Snow Pass (Mount Southington, Pleasant Mountain, Butternut, Blue Hills and others) and Mountain Collective were added from their own sites; Snow Triple Play (a ticket pack) and Freedom Pass (a perk for partner season-pass holders) are deliberately left out. "Independent" means on none of the five passes (e.g. Mad River Glen). The file holds coordinates, passes (`Epic`/`Ikon`/`Indy`/`Snow Pass`/`Mountain Collective`; `PASS_SHORT` in `src/lib/resorts.ts` has the short labels), summit/base/vertical/trails, and official `trailMapUrl`/`snowReportUrl`/`webcamUrl`. In Oct 2026 these links, trail counts and summit/base/vertical were looked up on each resort's own site for about 250 areas (every link loaded; a figure the resort didn't state, or that didn't add up, was left as the estimate). `estimates` lists fields that are uncertain; the UI marks them "est.". `opensOn` is this season's projected opening day (261 areas), from OnTheSnow's projected-openings pages per state and province (Oct 2026); the rest have no announced date. Refresh it every fall, along with passes. `src/lib/season.ts` uses it: before opening day there are no wind holds, and Decide greys out mountains that aren't open. Don't invent stats or URLs: look them up and flag anything unverified.
- `src/app/api/forecast/route.ts?region=…` fetches one region's daily summary from Open-Meteo (each location in its own time zone, in chunks of 100), in two parts: conditions now plus the next 7 days, cached 1 hour, and the past week plus days 8–16, cached 6 hours. `src/lib/forecast.ts` merges and parses them. This split keeps worst-case use near Open-Meteo's free limit (10,000 calls a day, 600 a minute; each location counts as a call, more for long ranges); if one part is refused, the route serves the other. More often than hourly, or any commercial use, needs Open-Meteo's paid plan. `AppState` loads regions as needed: the chosen one, your mountains' regions (for Home) and a resort page's own region.
- `src/app/api/resort/[id]/route.ts` is the heavy per-resort forecast, fetched only on a resort page: 72 hours at summit and base elevation, and 16 days from three models (GFS, ECMWF, GEM) for a confidence range. `src/lib/resortForecast.ts` parses it and derives rain/snow type and wind holds. Keep heavy data here, not in the all-resort summary.
- Weather service (`src/lib/nws.ts`), US only: free `api.weather.gov`, which asks every caller to send a User-Agent. `/api/alerts` fetches active alerts for every US state with a mountain in one request (cached 10 min) and matches them to each resort by its forecast zone or county, stored as `nws` in `resorts.json` (with the office; `src/data/nwsOffices.json` names each office's city). Only skier-relevant alerts (winter, wind, cold) are kept. Home shows one line per warning across your mountains; resort pages show the warning lines under the spec strip and the local office's forecaster discussion (key messages, else synopsis) folded away, via `/api/nws/[id]`. Times show in the mountain's own time zone.
- `src/lib/outlook.ts` makes Home's one-line note per mountain (powder day, next snow, flurries, or none). `src/lib/decide.ts` ranks resorts for a day (most snow, or closest) and estimates drive time from straight-line distance. Drive times are always labelled as estimates without traffic, and aren't shown past 12 hours (you'd fly).
- `src/components/AppState.tsx` is shared state across pages: forecasts, favorites, the passes you hold, and your starting point (saved in localStorage under the older `snowline:` keys, kept so saved lists survive), plus Explore's sort. `PassPicker` is one dropdown (a check per pass plus Independent, with All and Clear; nothing chosen means every mountain), used on Decide and Explore; `LocationSearch` edits the shared starting point.
- `src/components/SiteHeader.tsx` is the same plain bar on every tab: Snowbox wordmark on the snow ground over a 1px ink rule, tabs from tablet width up, and an optional `aside` (Explore puts its Map/List tabs there). Terrain imagery sits below it, never behind it.
- The Explore map is MapLibre (`ResortMap.tsx`), loaded client-side only, on Snowbox's own style (`QUIET_STYLE` in `src/lib/terrain.ts`): snow land, ice-blue water, relief, dashed state lines, state and city names only. Flat and north-up. Dots are colored by snow (`SNOW_BUCKETS` in `src/lib/format.ts`): next 7 days, or observed in the last 48 hrs, with a layer switch and key across the top of the map; your mountains are slightly larger and labelled with name and total; the selected dot gets an ink ring. No zoom buttons or hover pop-ups. Layers are added on `style.load` (not `load`, which a background tab can hold back).
- Observed snow and radar: `/api/observed` reads NOAA's National Snowfall Analysis (NOHRSC, `src/lib/observed.ts`), the newest 00/12 UTC 24- and 48-hour GeoTIFFs (values in inches; the NetCDF version is meters), sampled at each resort's cell and checked hourly. It covers the lower 48 only, so Canadian and Alaskan mountains have no observed value. The resort page's "Last 48 hrs" uses it when available (labelled as observed by NOAA, with the window's end), else the model. Radar is NOAA's national composite as tiles from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet (public domain, credited on the map), refreshed every 5 minutes. Explore's map switches between Next 7 days, Fell (48 hrs) and Radar.
- Live cams: `liveCams` in `resorts.json` lists YouTube live streams that the resort (or a partner it embeds, e.g. Mount Washington Observatory for Cranmore) publishes with embedding allowed; 8 cams at 6 resorts, checked Oct 2026. `/api/cams` re-checks them with YouTube's oEmbed every 6 hours so a restarted stream (new id) drops out instead of breaking. `LiveCam` shows YouTube's still until tapped, then plays muted in place. They show only on resort pages, under Snow; Home stays cam-free. Only embed cams whose owner allows it; other resorts' cams stay links. Mount Southington and Nashoba embed a channel live stream (only live in season), not yet added.
- Terrain: free, keyless sources in `src/lib/terrain.ts` (AWS Terrain Tiles elevation, OpenFreeMap basemap). Resort pages render a live 3D hero (`TerrainHero.tsx`, auto-aims at the high ground); Home uses a pre-rendered panorama of the Mount Mansfield range (`public/terrain/green-mountains.jpg`) whose sky is exactly the page color; re-render it the same way if it changes.
- Analytics: PostHog (`src/instrumentation-client.ts`, reads `NEXT_PUBLIC_POSTHOG_PROJECT_TOKEN`; events go through our `/ingest` path, rewritten to PostHog's US cloud in `next.config.ts`, so ad blockers don't drop them) and Statsig (`src/components/Statsig.tsx` around the app in the root layout, reads `NEXT_PUBLIC_STATSIG_CLIENT_KEY`, with the auto-capture web analytics plugin). Both are client-side only so pages stay pre-built, and each stays off when its key isn't set. Keys live in Vercel's environment variables, not in the code.
- Lifts and trails open: no free reliable source (Liftie blocks server requests; OnTheSnow's feed is paid), so resort pages link to the official report. Don't fake these numbers.

## Principles

- **No cards.** Drop the `sheet` surfaces (white, rounded, shadowed). Sections sit on the snow ground and are divided by rules: `1px solid #0f1a2a` between major sections, `1px solid #dfe5ec` between rows.
- **Blue means snow, nothing else.** Snow figures and bars use glacier `#1f5f8b` (darker than before), deep snow `#123f60`, light snow `#cfe1ee` / `#8fb7d4`, none `#cfd7e0`. Links use `#1f5f8b` too but only as text. Alpenglow `#e0532f` = powder day only. Barn red is no longer used for wind.
- **No pass colors.** Remove the Tailwind orange/blue/green dots everywhere (`PASS_COLORS`). Passes are text. On Home, a pass tag is filled ink when it's a pass the user holds (`myPasses`), outlined otherwise; resorts on no pass say "Independent".
- **Type does the work.** Archivo at narrow widths for anything that's a name or number: hero figures 92–104px at `font-stretch: 62%`, weight 800; names 17–19px at 75%, weight 700. Instrument Sans for reading text at 11/13/14/15px. Drop the "three sizes only" rule.
- **Controls are text, not chips.** Selected state = ink + bold + 2–3px ink underline. Filters read as a sentence with underlined tappable words.
- **Bottom tab bar:** text only (Home / Explore / Decide), Archivo 15px; active = weight 800 + 3px ink bar on top. No icons.
- **Wind hold:** wind line icon (stroke, ink) + the words "Wind hold possible". Never red, never an emoji.
- **Copy:** no captions that explain the UI ("Bars rise with…", "Red means…"). No fake timestamps. Sentence case. One plain sentence beats a styled box.
- **Header:** wordmark left, 1px ink rule under it.
